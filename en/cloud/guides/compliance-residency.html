Compliance and data residency: what to verify before renting

A provider's general certificate list is not proof that your specific service, region and data flow are covered.

← All cloud computeHardware home

Start with the data, not the logo

List what data enters the job: prompts, training data, checkpoints, logs, backups and support tickets. Mark personal data, sensitive business data and data subject to export control. Then map where each item is stored and processed.

Check region and service scope

Ask which region hosts the GPU, storage and control plane; whether support staff can access the data; and which certificates apply to that exact service. A provider may have SOC 2 for one product but not for the GPU cluster you rent.

Contract terms that matter

Look for data-retention periods, deletion proof, subprocessors, breach notification, government-access terms and cross-border transfer clauses. If data cannot leave a jurisdiction, confirm that backups and disaster recovery stay inside it too.

China-specific checks

For mainland workloads, verify CAC filing where applicable, cross-border data rules, local invoicing and whether the GPU provider is a reseller or the operator. “Direct access” from a mainland network is a reachability observation, not a compliance conclusion.

Keep an evidence file

Store the official certificate, its scope, the region, the contract clause and the verification date. Re-check when the service, region or contract changes; compliance is a moving target.

This is a buying checklist, not legal advice. Confirm with your compliance team and the provider's current attestations.
