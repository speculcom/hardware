Hardware · 2026 additions · 2026-11-26

Ten 2026 specs,
with capacity, bandwidth and interconnect kept apart

MN-Core L2000, Titania, Rubin, B300, MI455X, MI450X, TPU7x, Helios and Weiyun 1000Pro / 1000Ultra. They are often flattened into “how many TOPS”; this page separates per-GPU capacity, bandwidth, interconnect, rack and development stage.

Vendor spec pages only; peaks are not measurements, and design targets are not production. NVFP4 and MXFP4 are not cross-compared, and per-GPU is not mixed with rack.

Weiyun 1000Pro / 1000Ultra: 800B / 3.2T on one machine

2026-11-17Inference ASIC / development

PFN MN-Core L2000: targeting double the memory bandwidth

PFN and SEMIFIVE are co-developing the L2000 series, the next generation of AI inference chips, targeting more than twice the memory bandwidth of L1000. It continues the 3D-stacked logic + DRAM route, with tape-out planned for Q1 2027. It is not a shipping product; L1000 itself is planned for 2027.

Approach
3D 堆叠逻辑芯片 + DRAM；内存带宽优先
Target
内存带宽 > MN-Core L1000 两倍
Status
2027Q1 tape-out；尚未流片 / 上市
Partner
PFN + SEMIFIVE；逻辑芯片由三星代工
Why it matters

Inference is shifting from compute to memory bandwidth; PFN makes “double bandwidth” the first goal of L2000 rather than pushing TOPS. It is a useful architecture sample for non-GPU inference, 3D stacking and efficiency — but tape-out is in 2027, so it cannot inform a 2026 procurement decision.

Boundary / what it does not claim

“Double bandwidth” is a design target, not a measurement; tape-out is not volume production, and volume production is not a software ecosystem. PFN has not published TOPS, power, price or system specs. It does not claim to replace GPUs or to be purchasable in 2026.

Sources：PFN official news (EN) · PFN official news (JP)

2026-11-12Inference / chiplet

Axelera Titania: 2.5 PF FP8 / 128GB LPDDR6

A datacenter-class AIPU: two third-generation D-IMC chiplets in one SiP, 400W, 2.5 PFLOPS FP8; 128GB LPDDR6 at 2.45TB/s with 128MB shared SRAM; 32 RISC-V cores and multi-format codecs. Volume production is targeted for Q3 2027, with over $300M in orders claimed.

Form
2 chiplet / 1 SiP；PCIe AIC 或 OCP OAM

Memory
128GB LPDDR6；2.45TB/s；128MB 共享 SRAM
Power
400W 设计功耗
Status
2027Q3 量产目标；2026 仅发布架构
Why it matters

It fits 128GB per card and a 1.6T MoE into an air-cooled 4U eight-card server rather than a liquid-cooled rack; for teams with constrained facilities that still want a large local MoE, it is a different cost/power route from GPUs.

Boundary / what it does not claim

2.5 PF is FP8 peak while the 1.6T MoE figure is a capacity claim at FP4; they cannot be mixed. Orders and per-watt/TCO claims are vendor figures without independent measurement. In 2026 only the architecture is announced; silicon is targeted for Q3 2027, and software/model coverage remains unproven. It does not claim current availability or a general GPU replacement.

Sources：Axelera official announcement · Axelera product page

2026-11-12Accelerator

AMD MI450X: 288GB HBM4 / 23.3TB/s

Same CDNA5 and 23.3TB/s as MI455X, but 288GB (eight HBM4 stacks); MXFP4 40.3 PFLOPS; launched 2026-11-12.

Why it matters

It keeps MI455X bandwidth/compute at the 288GB tier, a more realistic capacity point for clusters that do not need 432GB; the difference is capacity, not architecture generation.

Boundary / what it does not claim

Do not assume MI455X is stronger just because the number is higher; per-GPU memory and rack configurations differ. MXFP4 and NVFP4 are not directly comparable.

2026-07-23Accelerator

AMD MI455X: 432GB HBM4 / 23.3TB/s

Per GPU: 432GB HBM4 (12 stacks), 23.3TB/s, 320B transistors; OCP MXFP4 40.3 PFLOPS, FP8 20.1 PFLOPS; UALoE 3.6TB/s; direct liquid cooling.

Why it matters

432GB is the largest publicly documented per-GPU capacity here; for keeping a very large model or long KV cache on one card, capacity decides feasibility before peak compute.

Boundary / what it does not claim

40.3 PF is OCP MXFP4 peak, not measured; it is not directly comparable with NVIDIA NVFP4. DLC means an air-cooled facility cannot simply take it.

Sources：AMD MI455X spec page

2026-04-22Accelerator / ASIC

Google TPU7x (Ironwood): 192GiB / 7.38TB/s

Seventh-generation TPU: 192GiB HBM3E per chip, about 7.38TB/s, 4614 TFLOPS FP8 / 2307 TFLOPS BF16; 9,216 chips per pod; ICI 1.2TB/s; JAX / PyTorch supported, TensorFlow not.

Why it matters

The most complete non-GPU option: per-chip capacity, pod scale and interconnect are public. For large-scale training with a JAX/PyTorch stack, it is a real alternative to GPUs.

Boundary / what it does not claim

Pod scale is not per-card throughput; 4614 TF is FP8 peak. The docs explicitly say TensorFlow is unsupported, so migration cost is separate. TPUs and GPUs cannot be compared through the same CUDA-ecosystem logic.

Sources：Google Cloud TPU7x docs

2026Accelerator

NVIDIA Rubin: 288GB HBM4 / 22TB/s

Per GPU: 288GB HBM4 (12-Hi stacks), 22TB/s, 336B transistors, 224 SM / 896 Tensor Cores; up to 50 PFLOPS NVFP4 inference; NVLink 6 at 3.6TB/s.

Why it matters

It lifts per-GPU bandwidth from 8TB/s to 22TB/s, a hard ceiling change for long-context and high-concurrency inference. It shares 288GB with B300 but differs in HBM generation and interconnect.

Boundary / what it does not claim

50 PF is NVFP4 inference, not every precision; it is not directly comparable with AMD MXFP4. It is a per-GPU figure, not the Vera Rubin NVL72 rack.

Sources：NVIDIA Rubin architecture blog

Why it matters

It is the “previous generation but mature” option still shipping in 2026; it shares 288GB with Rubin but differs in HBM generation, bandwidth and NVFP4 compute — a practical choice driven by supply and cost.

Boundary / what it does not claim

15 PF is dense NVFP4 and cannot be compared directly with Rubin’s 50 PF inference figure. 1,400W is max TGP; real power varies with workload.

2026System / rack

AMD Helios: 72× MI455X, 31TB HBM4

Rack-scale system: 72 MI455X or MI450X GPUs, 31TB HBM4, 1.4 EF FP8 / 2.9 EF FP4; not a per-GPU spec.

Why it matters

It answers how much HBM and compute one rack holds, the unit for cluster procurement and facility planning; kept separate from per-GPU entries so rack numbers are not mistaken for card numbers.

Boundary / what it does not claim

31TB is the sum of 72 cards; 1.4 EF FP8 and 2.9 EF FP4 are rack-level too. Power, cooling and facility conditions are separate; buying the rack is not a guarantee of peak runtime.
